\chapter{Planning Intelligence}
\label{chap:planning-intelligence}

\section{Overview and Scope}
\label{sec:pi-overview}

Planning Intelligence is the second, independent workspace of NetFix AI. Where the
Drive Test Intelligence workspace (\cref{chap:anomaly-detection}) turns raw
throughput measurements into prioritized incidents, Planning Intelligence solves a
different engineering problem: assigning radio identity parameters to every sector
of a live 5G NR network so that neighboring cells cannot be confused with one
another, while respecting the physical and combinatorial constraints imposed by
3GPP identity ranges.

\begin{infobox}{Scope of this chapter}
This chapter documents the NR5G PCI/RSI planning engine end to end: the
seven-stage pipeline, the deterministic assignment algorithms, the independent
validator, and the measured results of running the engine on a live Egyptian
Band~N41 deployment spanning \textbf{2{,}554 sectors} across \textbf{725 sites}
in four regions --- Alexandria (\texttt{ALX}), Sinai (\texttt{SIN}), Upper Egypt
(\texttt{UPP}), and Delta (\texttt{DEL}).
\end{infobox}

As noted in \cref{sec:pi-overview-business} of the project overview, the planning
dataset and the drive-test dataset are independent: planning output is never used
to prove or correct a drive-test anomaly, and drive-test observations are never
treated as ground truth for planning decisions.

\subsection{The Three Assigned Parameters}

Every sector requires three interrelated identity values, summarized in
\cref{tab:pi-parameters}.

\begin{table}[H]
  \centering
  \caption{Radio identity parameters assigned by the planning engine}
  \label{tab:pi-parameters}
  \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{l c Y}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Parameter} & \textbf{3GPP range} & \textbf{Role} \\
    \midrule
    PCI (Physical Cell Identity) & $0$--$1007$ & Primary identity broadcast by the cell; must not collide or be confused with nearby cells. Defined as $N_{ID}=3N_{ID}^{(2)}+N_{ID}^{(1)}$. \\
    RSI (Root Sequence Index) & $0$--$830$ & Determines the PRACH root sequence set; must not repeat within the reuse distance or random-access preambles collide. \\
    Mod4 $= \mathrm{PCI}\bmod 4$ & $0$--$3$ & Groups PCIs for SRS/DMRS orthogonality; four possible groups drive inter-site interference planning. \\
    Mod3 $= \mathrm{PCI}\bmod 3$ & $0$--$2$ & Groups PCIs for PSS/SSS synchronization-signal separation; enforced ring-adjacently on co-site sectors. \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabularx}
\end{table}

Mod3 and Mod4 are not independently assigned --- they are algebraic consequences
of the chosen PCI. However, the engine plans Mod4 \emph{before} PCI
(\cref{sec:pi-mod4}) and then searches for a PCI that both satisfies the hard
identity rules and lands on the already-chosen Mod4 group, since Mod4 has a
harder combinatorial ceiling (only 4 values) than Mod3 (3 values) against the
same neighbor density.

\subsection{Architecture Pivot: Full Reset vs. Incremental Planning}

Two architectural strategies were evaluated for re-planning the network:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Incremental (Option 2):} preserve every already-assigned PCI/RSI
    and patch only sectors that are flagged as requiring planning.
  \item \textbf{Full reset (Option 1):} discard every existing assignment and
    re-plan all 2{,}554 sectors from scratch under one consistent rule set.
\end{itemize}

Option 1 was chosen. A full reset removes path-dependent legacy artifacts
(assignments made under earlier, looser rules) and guarantees that the final
plan is provably consistent end to end, rather than consistent only with
respect to whichever subset happened to be replanned. All results in this
chapter are from a full-reset run.

\label{sec:pi-architecture}

The engine is implemented as nine flat, function-based Python modules --- no
classes, no dataclasses, plain dictionaries throughout the data flow. Each
module has exactly one responsibility, summarized in \cref{tab:pi-modules}.

\subsection{Why Regions Can Be Planned Independently}

\fname{bulk\_plan.py} splits the network into four regions using the
3-letter site-ID prefix (\texttt{region\_of()}). This is only correct because
analysis of the real network confirmed that \textbf{0\% of tier-1 (7\,km) and
tier-2 (14\,km) neighbor relationships cross a region boundary}. Coloring each
region's subgraph independently therefore gives exactly the same result as one
global coloring, at a fraction of the computational cost --- regions are
processed largest-first so that the most constrained (densest) subgraphs are
solved while the most wall-clock budget is still available.

\section{Pipeline Stages in Detail}
\label{sec:pi-stages}

\subsection{Stage 1: Data Loading}

\fname{load\_sectors()} reads the \texttt{Planning} sheet with \texttt{openpyxl},
validates every row, and returns sectors sorted deterministically by
\texttt{(site\_id, sector\_id)}. Validation checks latitude/longitude bounds,
azimuth $\in [0,360)$, PCI/RSI range membership, PCI/Mod4 arithmetic consistency,
and duplicate \texttt{site\_sector\_ID} detection --- every problem found is
reported at once rather than failing on the first error. A \texttt{plan\_all}
flag makes every unlocked sector a planning target regardless of the sheet's
\texttt{Requires\_Planning} column; this is what the full-reset architecture
uses instead of the sheet-flagged incremental mode.

    \bottomrule
  \end{tabularx}
\end{table}

Measured on the raw dataset: 2{,}554 sectors across 725 sites, all requiring
planning under the full-reset flag, single band \texttt{N41}.

\subsection{Stage 2: Neighbor Graph Construction}
\label{sec:pi-neighbor-graph}

Latitude/longitude is projected onto a local flat-earth East-North-Up (ENU)
plane centered on the dataset centroid --- accurate enough for cell-planning
distances (tens of kilometers) without a full geodesy library. A KD-tree
(\texttt{scipy.spatial.cKDTree}) then answers two radius queries:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Tier-1 (direct, 7\,km):} sectors that can physically see each
    other --- must not share PCI or RSI (collision).
  \item \textbf{Tier-2 (extended, 14\,km):} sectors within this wider radius
    must not share PCI either (confusion), since a UE could report the same PCI
    from two genuinely different physical cells; RSI reuse is also checked at
    this radius.
\end{itemize}

Neighbor lists are sorted by distance, and the whole graph is cached to disk
keyed on a hash of sector IDs, coordinates, and the two radii, so re-running on
an unchanged dataset skips the KD-tree rebuild entirely. Sectors co-located on
the same site are always mutual tier-1 neighbors at distance zero --- the
tightest possible reuse-distance case, and intentional.

\subsection{Stage 3: Geometry Engine}
\label{sec:pi-geometry}

\fname{facing.py} is shared verbatim by the assignment engine (Stage~5) and the
validator (Stage~6), which guarantees the planner and the independent auditor
can never disagree about what ``facing'' means. Every function is
parameter-free: no tuned beamwidth cutoff or arbitrary threshold constant is
used anywhere in the geometry model.

\begin{align}
  \texttt{bearing\_deg}(A,B) &= \text{great-circle compass bearing from } A \text{ to } B \\
  \texttt{angle\_diff}(a,b) &= \min(|a-b| \bmod 360,\; 360 - (|a-b| \bmod 360)) \\
  \texttt{distance\_m}(A,B) &= \text{haversine great-circle distance} \\
  \texttt{alignment}(A,B) &= \max\!\big(0,\ \cos(\texttt{angle\_diff}(\text{azimuth}_A,\ \texttt{bearing\_deg}(A,B)))\big) \label{eq:alignment}\\
  \texttt{interference\_potential}(A,B) &= \frac{\texttt{alignment}(A,B) + \texttt{alignment}(B,A)}{\texttt{distance\_m}(A,B)^2} \label{eq:interference}
\end{align}

\Cref{eq:alignment} returns $0$ for co-located sectors (bearing undefined) or
angular offsets $\geq 90^\circ$, and $1$ for a dead-ahead offset of $0^\circ$.
\Cref{fig:pi-alignment-curve} plots this relationship.

\projectfigure{PI_02_cosine_alignment_model.png}
  {Cosine alignment model: alignment vs. angular offset from boresight. Key points:
  $0^\circ \rightarrow 1.0$, $60^\circ \rightarrow 0.5$, $90^\circ \rightarrow 0.0$.}
  {fig:pi-alignment-curve}

\projectfigure{PI_03_interference_potential_vs_distance.png}
  {Interference potential vs. distance for a fully-facing pair (both alignments
  $=1.0$) and a one-sided pair (only one antenna facing). Tier-1 (7\,km) and
  Tier-2 (14\,km) boundaries are marked for reference.}
  {fig:pi-interference-distance}

\begin{infobox}{Why not a binary cone?}
A hard beamwidth cutoff (e.g.\ ``within $60^\circ$ = facing, beyond = not'') creates
a discontinuity with no physical basis: a neighbor at $59^\circ$ and one at
$61^\circ$ would be treated as completely different cases despite nearly
identical radiated energy in that direction. The cosine model in
\cref{eq:alignment} degrades smoothly instead. It is also deliberately
parameter-free --- no beamwidth or threshold constant had to be chosen or tuned,
which matters because \colname{cell\_radius} in the available dataset is a
uniform placeholder rather than per-site RF data, so any tuned cutoff would have
been arbitrary rather than derived.
\end{infobox}

\subsection{Stage 4: Constraint Generation}
\label{sec:pi-constraints}

\fname{constraints.py} answers exactly one question: \emph{which PCI and RSI
values are legal for a given sector, right now?} It never mutates assignments;
it only computes forbidden and preferred sets from the current state.

\paragraph{Ring adjacency.} Co-site sectors are ordered by azimuth and treated
as a ring; \texttt{ring\_adjacent\_ids()} returns each sector's two immediate
neighbors in that ring. This single helper backs both the Mod3 rule below and
the co-site Mod4 rule in \cref{sec:pi-mod4}.

\paragraph{Site-size-aware Mod3 rule.} Because only 3 Mod3 values exist, the
rule strictness depends on how many sectors share a site:

\begin{table}[H]
  \centering
  \caption{Mod3 rule by site size}
  \label{tab:pi-mod3-rule}
  \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{l Y}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Site size} & \textbf{Mod3 rule} \\
    \midrule
    $1$--$3$ co-located sectors & Every pair must differ (3 values are always sufficient for $\leq 3$ sectors). \\
    $4$--$5$ co-located sectors & Ring-adjacent pairs must differ; all 3 Mod3 values should appear on the site; any unavoidable reuse falls only on non-adjacent pairs. \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabularx}
\end{table}

site.

\section{Stage 5: The Assignment Engine}
\label{sec:pi-assignment}

\fname{assignment\_engine.py} runs three sub-stages in strict order: Mod4, then
PCI, then RSI. Every hard rule from Stage~4 is enforced without exception; the
only freedom the engine has is in \emph{which} legal value to pick, and in
which \emph{order} sectors are processed.

\subsection{Sub-stage 5.1: Mod4 Assignment}
\label{sec:pi-mod4}

Mod4 is planned as a \textbf{weighted graph-coloring problem}. Nodes are
sectors; edges connect tier-1 inter-site pairs with nonzero
\texttt{interference\_potential} (\cref{eq:interference}); edge weight is that
potential. Co-site pairs are excluded from this graph and handled by a separate
co-site rule.

\paragraph{Co-site Mod4 rule.} Analogous to the Mod3 rule but with only 4
values available:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Sites with $\leq 4$ sectors: every sibling pair must have a different Mod4.
  \item Sites with 5 sectors: only ring-adjacent siblings must differ (full
    pairwise separation is mathematically impossible: 5 sectors into 4 colors).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Step 1 --- DSATUR initial coloring.} The engine colors sectors using
the degree-of-saturation (DSATUR) heuristic, adapted to a weighted objective.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Weighted DSATUR coloring for Mod4}
\label{alg:pi-dsatur}
\KwIn{sectors to plan, interference edges, co-site forbidden-color function}
\KwOut{mod4\_of: mapping from sector id to $\{0,1,2,3\}$}
Initialize \texttt{uncolored} $\leftarrow$ all sectors to plan\;
\While{\texttt{uncolored} is not empty}{
  Select $s^\ast = \arg\max_{s \in \texttt{uncolored}}$ (number of distinct
  colors already used by $s$'s neighbors, tie-broken by total incident edge
  weight, then by sector id)\;
  Compute \texttt{forbidden} $\leftarrow$ co-site forbidden colors for $s^\ast$\;
  \texttt{candidates} $\leftarrow \{0,1,2,3\} \setminus \texttt{forbidden}$ (or all 4, if that is empty)\;
  Choose $c^\ast = \arg\min_{c \in \texttt{candidates}}$ (weighted same-color clash if $s^\ast$ took color $c$), tie-broken deterministically by an MD5 hash of $(s^\ast, c)$\;
  Assign \texttt{mod4\_of}$[s^\ast] \leftarrow c^\ast$; remove $s^\ast$ from \texttt{uncolored}\;
}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Step 2 --- Local search repair.} DSATUR alone does not reach a local
optimum for a weighted objective, so a repair pass follows.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Weighted local search repair (recolor + swap)}
\label{alg:pi-repair}
\KwIn{initial mod4\_of, interference edges, co-site legality rules, time budget (\SI{60}{\second})}
\KwOut{repaired mod4\_of}
\Repeat{no improving move exists \textnormal{or} time budget exceeded}{
  \texttt{clashing} $\leftarrow$ sectors with nonzero same-color weight, sorted worst-first\;
  \ForEach{$s \in \texttt{clashing}$}{
    Evaluate every legal \textbf{recolor} of $s$ to another color\;
    Evaluate every legal \textbf{swap} of $s$'s color with a co-site sibling's color\;
  }
  Apply the single move (recolor or swap) with the most negative total-weight delta\;
}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{successbox}{Termination guarantee}
Total interference weight is bounded below by zero and \emph{strictly}
decreases every round that a move is applied, so the repair loop is guaranteed
to terminate at a genuine local optimum. The \SI{60}{\second} wall-clock budget
in \cref{alg:pi-repair} is a safety net for exceptionally dense pockets, not the
expected termination path.
\end{successbox}

\subsection{Sub-stage 5.2: PCI Assignment}

With Mod4 fixed, \texttt{assign\_pci\_rsi()} processes sectors
\textbf{site-by-site in azimuth (ring) order, hardest sites first} (ranked by
the maximum tier-2 degree of any sector on the site), so that Mod3
site-coverage state can be tracked incrementally as siblings are placed one
after another. For each sector, the candidate PCI pool is filtered through the
hard rules --- no tier-1 collision, no tier-2 confusion, not in a forbidden
Mod3 group --- and then narrowed by the four-layer preference funnel in
\cref{tab:pi-pci-layers}.

\begin{table}[H]
  \centering
  \caption{PCI layered preference funnel}
  \label{tab:pi-pci-layers}
  \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{c Y}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Layer} & \textbf{Preference} \\
    \midrule
    1 (ideal) & Matches the sector's already-assigned Mod4 \emph{and} fills a Mod3 value still missing on the site \\
    2 & Matches assigned Mod4 only \\
    3 & Fills a missing Mod3 value only \\
    4 (fallback) & Any hard-legal candidate \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabularx}
\end{table}

Within whichever layer yields a non-empty pool, the PCI with the lowest global
usage count is preferred; ties are broken with a deterministic MD5 hash of
$(\text{sector id}, \text{salt})$ so that re-running the pipeline on unchanged
input always reproduces \emph{exactly} the same plan --- an explicit design
requirement, verified empirically (\cref{sec:pi-results}) by running the full
pipeline twice and diffing the outputs.

\subsection{Sub-stage 5.3: RSI Assignment}

RSI has no per-site structural rule --- only global reuse-distance pressure ---
so sectors are processed \textbf{hardest-first by tier-2 neighbor count}
instead of by site/azimuth order. Candidates exclude every RSI already used by
a tier-2 neighbor. If that leaves nothing (a structurally dense pocket), the
engine falls back to the least-conflicted RSI across the full range and logs a
warning rather than raising an error, since exhaustive tier-2 exclusion failing
indicates network density, not a bug. Ties are broken by least global usage,
then the same deterministic hash used for PCI.

\begin{infobox}{Why RSI reuse could be promoted to a hard rule}
A degree-bound proof over the measured tier-2 neighbor counts
(\cref{tab:pi-graph-stats}) confirmed that even the worst-case 14\,km
neighborhood --- 344 sectors, the maximum observed --- still leaves more than
480 of the 831 available RSI values free. This headroom is what justifies
treating RSI Tier-2 reuse as a \emph{hard} constraint rather than a soft one:
the network is never so dense that zero-reuse RSI assignment becomes
infeasible.
\end{infobox}

\section{Stage 6: Independent Validation}
\label{sec:pi-validation}

\fname{validator.py} recomputes every hard rule \textbf{completely
independently} of the planner --- it never reads planner-internal state, only
the final \texttt{\{pci, rsi, mod4\}} assignments, and it re-derives Mod3/Mod4
from PCI itself rather than trusting whatever the engine wrote. It reuses
\fname{facing.py} from Stage~3, so the planner and the validator can never
disagree about geometry. Every metric is reported at three granularities ---
by pair, by sector, and by site --- and the overall \texttt{pass} flag is
\texttt{True} only when \emph{every} hard-rule count is exactly zero.

\begin{table}[H]
  \centering
  \caption{Rules checked by the independent validator}
  \label{tab:pi-validator-rules}
  \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{l c Y}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Rule} & \textbf{Hard/Soft} & \textbf{Detection method} \\
    \midrule
    PCI Collision & Hard & Any tier-1 pair sharing PCI \\
    PCI Confusion & Hard & Any tier-2 pair sharing PCI \\
    RSI Reuse & Hard & Any tier-2 pair sharing RSI \\
    Mod3 Adjacent & Hard & Ring-adjacent co-site pair sharing Mod3 \\
    Mod3 Rule Violations & Hard & 4--5 sector site not using all 3 Mod3 values \\
    Mod4 Intra-site & Hard & Ring-adjacent co-site pair sharing Mod4 \\
    Mod3 Non-Adj Reuse & Soft & Non-adjacent co-site pair sharing Mod3 \\
    Mod4 Non-Adj Intra-site & Soft & Non-adjacent co-site pair sharing Mod4 \\
    Mod4 Inter-site & Soft & Sector's nearest facing inter-site neighbor shares Mod4 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The Mod4 Inter-site check deserves special mention: it is \textbf{per-sector
and directed}, matching the definition used by the reference clash sheet this
engine was built against. For sector $S$, find the nearest tier-1 neighbor on a
different site that $S$'s antenna actually points at
($\texttt{alignment}(S,N) > 0$), and count one clash if
$\text{Mod4}(S) = \text{Mod4}(N)$. Its maximum possible value therefore equals
the total sector count (2{,}554), not the pair count.

\section{Stage 7: Export}

\fname{exporter.py} writes \texttt{PCI}/\texttt{RSI}/\texttt{Mod4} back into the
source workbook's \texttt{Planning} sheet by matching rows on
\texttt{site\_sector\_ID} --- never by row position, since row order is not
guaranteed to match assignment order --- and appends a \texttt{KPI\_Report}
sheet generated directly from the validator's report dictionary. There is no
separate calculation path for the exported numbers, so the numbers a reviewer
sees in the spreadsheet and the numbers the validator computed can never drift
apart.

\section{Soft Rules Reference}
\label{sec:pi-soft-rules}

Each soft rule exists because the network's own density creates a pigeonhole
constraint --- not because the planner failed to try. \Cref{tab:pi-soft-rules}
reports measured values alongside the structural expectation each should match.

minimized as far as that local search can reach rather than eliminated outright.

\begin{warningbox}{Mod4 inter-site is a structural ceiling --- not an algorithm limitation}
Single-sector tabu-style local search plateaus almost immediately at
$\sim$7\,km neighborhood density: with $\sim$100 tier-1 neighbors competing for
only 4 colors, there are simply not enough degrees of freedom for any
single-sector move to eliminate every clash. Further reduction requires
\emph{coordinated multi-sector} moves --- changing several sectors' colors
together in a way that no single-sector move can discover on its own. This is
an identified direction for future work rather than a defect in the current
repair pass.
\end{warningbox}

\section{Chapter Summary}
\label{sec:pi-summary}

The Planning Intelligence engine assigns PCI, RSI, and Mod4/Mod3 to every
sector of a 2{,}554-sector, 725-site live network through a seven-stage,
flat-function pipeline. Mod4 is solved as a weighted graph-coloring problem
(DSATUR plus guaranteed-terminating local search repair); PCI is chosen through
a four-layer preference funnel that respects Mod4 and Mod3 simultaneously; RSI
is assigned through a global tier-2 exclusion scheme justified by a
degree-bound feasibility proof. An independent validator, sharing only the
geometry module with the planner, confirmed \textbf{zero hard-rule violations}
across the entire dataset, and the two soft-rule metrics that have exact
structural expectations matched them precisely. The remaining soft metric ---
Mod4 inter-site clashes --- represents a genuine, density-driven structural
ceiling for single-sector optimization, and is the basis for the multi-sector
coordinated-move extension proposed as future work in
\cref{chap:integration-platform-design}.
